The Implicit Brain Model is a multimodal foundation model of whole-brain state and dynamics. One parameter set is declared once, and task-specific models are materialized out of it on demand. Because the representation is tied to the brain rather than to an instrument, film, MEG, EEG and motion capture all constrain the same parameters — data that could not otherwise be combined at all.

One Declaration, Forty-Four Models

Fields carry physical state on a support, topologies say which state may interact, and processes are dynamics over a topology. A model names regions, a resolution and a bandwidth; dependency tracing instantiates only what that request actually reaches, so the same field can sit at 1 mm across the brain and 50 µm around one electrode.

M = materialize(R, r, B, F, A, T, P)

Control: a kernel with its site rows permuted holds the body up just as well. The cortex is the motor owner; what it learned has not been shown to contribute.

Predicting How TMS Alters the Brain

Place a coil, get the cortex it actually reaches. The pulse is 100 µs; the induced E-field is solved through the head volume at high resolution, because the skull and scalp matter here as much as the brain does. What comes back is the evoked potential a real montage would record.

  • 100 µspulse, resolved in the head volume under the coil and coarse in the brain — the resolution follows the physics, not a uniform grid.
  • E-field → TEPcoil pose and current to induced field to the potential evoked at each contact, in one traced model.
  • phantom-checkeda TEP is notoriously reproducible from the stimulation artifact alone, so the artifact phantoms are a declared negative control rather than an afterthought.

Why the head volume: unlike electrical stimulation, a magnetic pulse does not care about scalp impedance — it induces where the field curls, so the geometry of skull and CSF decides which gyrus fires.

Predicting Long-Term Spectral Reorganization

The question a course of treatment is actually chosen on: not what one pulse does, but how the spectrum of a region reorganises over weeks of them. Plasticity is declared over the hippocampal and neuromodulatory systems, so a protocol can be run forward and the band power it lands on read off.

  • weeks, not millisecondsthe slow variable is synaptic, so the model integrates a protocol rather than replaying a single response.
  • per-region spectraband power before and after, on the region the protocol targeted rather than on the whole head.
  • against persistencespectra drift on their own, so the bar is beating "predicts no change" by more than the test–retest spread.

Why this is the one that matters: a model that predicts the acute response tells you the coil is on. A model that predicts the reorganisation tells you whether the course is working.
